The Agora Sample conversation

Why am I never satisfied?

“Even when something goes well, I’m already thinking about what’s missing. How do I know when enough is enough?”

An illustrative anonymous question · Three perspectives

SenecaOn knowing what is enough

Give “enough” a meaning of your own.

You are not starved of things; you are starved of a standard. Decide what enough means before the world decides for you.

Notice which desires return even when you satisfy them. If every achievement merely moves the threshold, the threshold deserves your attention. What would a good ordinary day contain, if no one else could judge it?

NietzscheOn choosing what to want

Examine the object of your hunger.

Perhaps the question is not how to quiet your wanting, but whether what you want is worthy of you.

Some dissatisfaction asks you to change. Some simply repeats the ambitions you have borrowed. Ask which pursuit you would still choose without applause, and whether your current restlessness is leading you towards it.

CamusOn living with incompleteness

A life can be unfinished and still be yours.

You may never reach a moment in which everything feels settled. That does not make the present a waiting room.

There is room to desire something more and still inhabit this afternoon. What is already here that your search has made difficult to notice?

Where they differ

Set a limit, choose a direction, or make room for the unfinished?

Seneca asks you to define enough. Nietzsche challenges what you are reaching for. Camus questions whether satisfaction must arrive before life can begin. They leave you with different tasks, rather than a single answer.

Another question in the Agora

Do I owe the world my attention?

Hannah Arendt might ask what public responsibilities your attention makes possible. Seneca might ask which demands are within your power to answer. Camus might begin with the person whose suffering you can actually help relieve.

The unresolved question: where does protecting your attention become withdrawing from a responsibility you could meet?
